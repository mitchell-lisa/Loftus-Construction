## What is broken about the current site

1. **The site is hacked.** `index.php` carries a hidden block, `<div id="xblock" style="display: none;">`, containing a link to a stolen-card tutorial site, with an inline script that re-hides it using a split string: `document.getElementById("x"+"block").style.display="no"+"ne"`. A second injected link to another spam domain sits on `pre-construction.php`. This is textbook cloaked SEO spam on a compromised PHP install. It is the single most serious finding and it is visible to anyone who views source, including an agency evaluating them.
2. **No HTTPS.** Port 443 resets the connection in a browser; the request that does resolve redirects back down to `http://`. Every visitor sees "Not secure" in the address bar. For a firm whose clients are PennDOT, the PA Turnpike Commission, NJ Transit and the City of Philadelphia, that is a credibility problem before anyone reads a word.
3. **Content frozen in 2019.** The newest item on the news page is dated September 2019. Meanwhile they are mid-contract on a $2.3M bridge as of August 2026. The site actively understates the company.
4. **No SEO fundamentals.** No meta description on any page. No `<h1>` on any page. No Open Graph tags. No structured data. `sitemap.xml` returns 404. Page titles are the identical string "Loftus Construction, Inc." on all twelve pages.
5. **Not mobile-usable in any real sense.** `<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width">` with no `initial-scale`. Built on Zurb Foundation 4 with Zepto, a framework generation from roughly 2013.
6. **Copy errors in the shipped text.** `services.php` says "Build-Design" where every other page says "Design-Build." The news page has "(8) Bridges masonry arch bridges," a doubled word, and a sentence ending in "..". The homepage uses a mathematical minus sign in "Design−Build" instead of a hyphen.
7. **A 404 on the homepage.** One resource fails to load on every visit.
8. **Photography is real but tiny.** The six capability photographs are 350 by 260 pixels. The homepage sliders are 1140 by 355. The only team photograph is a 2019 banquet snapshot with a heavy purple colour cast, out of focus, taken in a ballroom.
9. **Credit line for the previous designer** sits in the footer of every page.

## Category conventions, from six comparable sites

Read: [Clearwater Construction](https://www.clearwaterconstruction.com/), [Wagman](https://www.wagman.com/specialized-services/bridges-and-structures/), [Crisdel](https://www.crisdel.com/), [Earle](https://earleco.com/divisions/heavy-civil-construction/), [J.D. Eckman](https://jdeckman.com/), [Allan Myers](https://www.allanmyers.com/).

What the category actually does, consistently:

- Navigation is About, Capabilities or Services, Projects, Careers, Contact. Nobody calls it "Products." Safety is a top-level or second-level item on most of them.
- The founding year is stated in or near the hero. Eckman: "Family-Owned and Employee-Focused…since 1945." Wagman: "Over 120 years."
- Projects are the portfolio, named individually, with the owner agency attached. Contract value is shown by some and omitted by others.
- Prequalification status is stated plainly as a credential.
- Association logos sit in the footer as a credibility grid.
- Careers is prominent and is treated as a primary audience, not an afterthought. This category competes for engineers as hard as it competes for contracts.
- Nobody uses lead-capture forms as the primary call to action. The call to action is a phone number and a named person.

Loftus already has the strongest raw material of any of these: named clients, a real award, a specific prequalification list, and a genuinely unusual specialism in historic masonry arch rehabilitation. None of it is presented.
